Resource development

From geological information to informed decisions

The strength of a resource conversation depends on what the evidence can support, where its limits lie and which question comes next.

AGANO insights · 19 October 2026

An observation is a starting point

Geological information can come from many activities, including mapping, sampling, surveys and drilling. The existence of information does not make every conclusion equally well supported. The question is what the information actually establishes and how it relates to the wider setting.

Understand how the information was produced

The usefulness of technical information depends on its origin, the methods used, the way it was recorded and the checks applied. A clear account of those matters helps readers understand what can be relied on and what still requires investigation.

Separate stages of understanding

An early indication, a technical interpretation and a formally reported estimate serve different purposes. They should not be presented as interchangeable. Dates, classifications, units and source documents provide context that a headline figure cannot carry on its own.

Connect the geology to practical requirements

Resource opportunities also raise questions about access, infrastructure, material handling, processing and the operating setting. Geological interest is only one part of that discussion. A considered evaluation recognises where different disciplines and additional information are needed.

Make the next question specific

Progress becomes more useful when the next investigation has a clear purpose. A concise introduction should state the commodity, the general location, the stage of work and the nature of the discussion being sought. Confidential files and sensitive coordinates can be reserved for a suitable channel.
